# Audit checklist and synthetic example

For every substantive finding check: (1) exact excerpt and location; (2) intended speaker, negation, modality, population, comparator and time; (3) strongest matching method/result/table or external source; (4) alternate interpretation and qualification elsewhere; (5) why the inference is or is not supported; (6) proportional severity; (7) a specific repair. Preserve checked/no finding, unverified and not-applicable facets, and page/paragraph coverage. Review input documents in full; do not call an extraction index a scientific review.

Synthetic example only: manuscript line 1 says “The observational sample demonstrated X causes Y.” Its Methods line 2 says “We measured X and Y once, without intervention.” A justified medium/high concern (depending on centrality) is a causal inference stronger than this design, not that association is absent. Suggest “X was associated with Y in this sample,” conditional on the actual results. If surrounding text labels the statement as another author's hypothesis, reassess and do not accuse the manuscript of making the claim. Source excerpts and methods must actually exist in the case.

For figures, verify table units, populations and direction against original caption/table and report exact page/table/cell. For an inaccessible external reference, record the source and unresolved support; do not label it disproved. Out-of-scope inferences remain marked as such. If a finding relies on a scanned page, attach a page locator and visual verification rather than claiming the automatic check succeeded.
